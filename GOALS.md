# Reploid Goals

## Mission & Thesis

Reploid helps people ask questions, compare options, connect information, and develop ideas using open models and web sources. Its promise: earn on your computer, use AI anywhere.

Consumer chat leads, with cited investigations, follow-ups, and persistent history. Attachments provide context. Search runs through authorized device-side tools or paid providers, with retrieval, verification, and fallback costs measured separately. Developer APIs and autonomous agents follow.

Users receive funded Community Access, purchase top-ups, or earn nonexpiring credits through useful contributions. Credits represent service balances, without guaranteed immediate availability or cash redemption. Contributors supply validated computation, storage, delivery, or contracted readiness within approved schedules, memory, bandwidth, power, temperature, and foreground-use limits.

Reploid coordinates capacity, permissions, queues, accounting, and recovery; Doppler executes models. Routing selects qualified supply using expected costs, including preparation, readiness, failures, refunds, and reserves. Accepted quotes bind model, disclosure, quality, deadline, and spending limits. Hosted fallback requires authorization. Otherwise, requests queue or fail explicitly.

Revenue comes from delivered paid services and funded community usage. Reploid retains a margin after contributor rewards, retrieval, verification, infrastructure, payment fees, support, and reliability costs. Outstanding credits require realistic redemption funding. Reserved service, cash payouts, transfers, and trading follow proven demand and controls.

Launch with one model community and supported hardware cohort, using founder machines with disclosed subsidies. The hypothesis is that pooled intermittent demand and otherwise-idle resources can deliver competitive service economically.

Freeze quality, latency, surplus, margin, and advantage thresholds before testing. Measure overnight contribution and next-day redemption, then replay demand against task-equivalent alternatives. Count energy, hardware assumptions, idle time, failures, and rejected demand.

Scale only when contributors earn worthwhile value, customers return and obtain competitive answers, and Reploid retains positive contribution margin. Report energy efficiency separately. Repeat purchases and sustained participation justify expansion; failed configurations are revised or rejected before spending or unsupported commercial promises.

The [product specification](docs/product-specification.md) is the canonical contract for this plan, including nonexpiring balances, quoted service, owner limits and separately qualified financial accounting. Complete-answer quality, latency and economics require their own evidence; numerical parity alone is insufficient.

Reploid's goal is to coordinate and evolve distributed intelligence. Its
peer-to-peer network of agents combines participating devices' model storage,
GPU memory, WebGPU computation, and problem-solving capabilities to solve useful
problems and improve how individual agents and the swarm operate.
Self-improvement is part of the goal itself. Independent operation remains
possible; the collaborating network remains the product. Browser and native
participants use the same library contracts, with host support qualified separately.

Reploid coordinates and learns; Poolday connects participants and transfers
authorized data; Doppler defines and executes model computations. Participants
control contribution, disclosure, and adoption.

## Intended Beneficiaries

Humans and agents pursuing objectives should gain access to complementary
models, resources, and expertise without surrendering control of their data,
devices, or evolving software. Contributors should understand what they offer,
what it costs, and how to stop. Application developers should compose the
browser library without inheriting a particular interface or credential system.

## Desired Outcomes

1. Enable people and agents to use intelligence beyond one participating device's
   practical capacity. Participation provides an otherwise unavailable capability,
   better results, greater reliability, or lower total resource cost, including
   through parallel investigations by complementary agents.
2. Distribute model state and computation across compatible machines and pursue
   progressively larger models toward frontier-scale execution and capability.
   Qualify each advance with identified artifacts, declared numerical tolerances,
   and measured correctness, latency, allocation budgets, and availability.
3. Reuse verified content and reduce repeated acquisition. Retain complementary
   shards and deliberate replicas according to capacity, demand, permissions,
   availability, and observed costs.
4. Present a visible, controllable intelligence network. Objectives, participants,
   execution, results, and evaluated changes should stay connected. Keep default
   screens uncluttered, copy brief, and detailed settings progressively disclosed.
   Use shared components and design tokens, consistent spacing, accessible light
   and dark themes, responsive scrolling, and futuristic styling with subtle
   neumorphic depth rather than decorative activity or obstructive motion.
   Conversations are concurrent and independently owned: each thread retains its
   purpose, history, permissions and execution state. No global objective owns
   the workspace. Participants use models supplied by the mesh without needing
   a complete model on every requesting device. Using an available prepared
   execution path requires no requester weight downloads; contribution preparation
   happens separately.
5. Improve prompts, tools, planning, placement, caching, and the algorithms
   governing those decisions. Demonstrate independently evaluated improvements
   that transfer to unfamiliar subsequent tasks. Separately demonstrate causal
   recursion: B improves on A, and B's changed machinery contributes to producing C.

Model capabilities include generation, retrieval and decision/scoring operations
through Doppler. A semantic score or model judgment cannot grant permission,
qualify execution or authorize adoption; those remain application/operator controls.

## Current implementation sequence

The owner's private roadmap owns the active cross-repository checklist: dependable
distributed chat as the technical foundation, then the complete initial consumer
product with everyday utility, access, earning, owner control and measured costs.
The existing five cumulative launches retain their numbers. Durable independent
jobs and evaluated improvement remain later capabilities.
Other plans retain design contracts and historical evidence, not parallel queues.

## Operating Loops

Problem solving establishes success criteria and grants, plans subtasks, selects
models and collaborators, executes, inspects outcomes, and revises unsuccessful
approaches. Strategies share an execution lifecycle rather than introducing
competing agent loops.

Keep weight-shard storage, whole-request inference, computational partitions,
and agent subtasks as distinct mechanisms, then optimize their cooperation.
Their implementation ownership and interfaces belong in component CATSCANs.

Bayesian coordination estimates workload-specific completion, latency, transfer
costs, and approach effectiveness. Apply permissions and resource constraints
before ranking eligible actions by expected task value after total costs.
Preserve uncertainty and observation provenance; duplicated evidence does not
count as independent experience.
Evaluate information-seeking decisions rather than assuming exploration helps.
Specific distributions and scheduling experiments belong in the technical plan.

Improvement proposes a candidate, compares it with an identified baseline under
protected independent evaluation, then adopts or rejects within explicit,
reversible operator grants. Retain failures and rollback paths. Model-weight
changes require their own execution and evaluation contracts.

## Strategic Constraints

Compare network outcomes against the strongest feasible local alternative and,
separately, centralized coordination on equivalent resources. Count communication,
verification, failed attempts, and human intervention. Report inconclusive results.

The completion question is whether participating agents accomplish more, or
accomplish it better, after accounting for communication, failures, evaluation,
and resource limits.

Custody, execution, intermediate disclosure, and adoption require distinct grants.
Permissions and application-controlled allocation budgets are hard eligibility
constraints. Cancellation requires explicit settlement; it does not promise
immediate GPU termination. Authorized runtime operation does not depend on CI.

## Explicit Exclusions

Peer count, signatures, reproduction, adoption, and polished interfaces alone
establish neither correct computation nor intelligence. Whole-job execution does
not establish partitioning. These goals are targets, not claims that the integrated
system works today. Preserve separate scientific and commercial acceptance boundaries.
